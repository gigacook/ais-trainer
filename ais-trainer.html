    The exam

    OverviewThe worksheet at a glance
    Core

    Per side: 10 key muscles graded 0–5 and 28 dermatomes scored 0–2 for light touch and pin prick, plus the anorectal exam (VAC, DAP). The output is levels, an AIS grade and the ZPP.

    Body

    The exam is done supine. The worksheet mirrors the patient: right side on the left of the page, left side on the right, segments C2 to S4-5 down the middle.

    Classification runs in a fixed order, and each step uses only what came before: sensory levels → motor levels → NLI → complete or incomplete → AIS grade → ZPP.

    The subscores (UEMS, LEMS, LT, PP totals) track change over time. They never set the AIS grade.

    What ifs
    What if part of the exam can't be done?

    Score that item NT. If it blocks a step, record that result as ND (not determinable).

    What if the patient had a nerve problem before the injury?

    Tag the affected scores with an asterisk and explain in the comments box whether they count as normal for classification.

    GradingMuscle grades 0–5
    Core

    Grade 3 = full range against gravity. It's the threshold that defines the motor level and separates AIS C from D. Grade 5 is normal.

    Body
    GradeMeaning
    0Total paralysis
    1Palpable or visible contraction
    2Active movement, full range of motion with gravity eliminated
    3Active movement, full range against gravity
    4Full range against gravity and moderate resistance in a muscle-specific position
    5Normal: full range against gravity and full resistance, as expected in an unimpaired person
    NTNot testable: immobilization, severe pain, amputation, or contracture over 50% of normal range
    0*–4*Non-SCI condition present; explain in comments
    What ifs
    What if the muscle moves only part of the range against gravity?

    It's not a 3. Full range is required. If it completes the full range with gravity eliminated, it's a 2.

    What if a contracture blocks more than half the range?

    Score NT. The grade can't be judged.

    What if weakness comes from an old brachial plexus injury?

    Score what you find with an asterisk (e.g. 3*) and state in comments whether it should be treated as normal for classification.

    GradingSensory scores 0–2
    Core

    Each dermatome gets two scores, light touch and pin prick: 0 absent, 1 altered, 2 normal. A dermatome is intact only when both are 2.

    Body

    Sensation is compared with the face (cheek) as the normal reference.

    • 1 = altered: decreased, impaired, or hypersensitive.
    • Pin prick tests sharp versus dull. If the patient can't reliably tell sharp from dull, PP = 0.
    • Light touch uses a cotton tip stroked over no more than 1 cm.

    Each modality totals 56 per side, 112 for both.

    What ifs
    What if touch is normal but the pin feels dull?

    LT 2, PP 0 (or 1 if sharp is felt but different). The dermatome is not intact, so the sensory level stops above it.

    What if light touch feels painful?

    Hypersensitivity is altered sensation: score 1.

    What if the dermatome is under a cast?

    Score NT.

    No testable key muscle. The motor level there is taken from the sensory level.

    ExamKey sensory points
    Core

    Body
    LevelPoint
    What ifs
    What if T7–T9 are hard to find?

    On the midclavicular line, split the T6 (xiphisternum) to T10 (umbilicus) distance into quarters: T7, T8, T9.

    ExamAnorectal exam: VAC and DAP
    Core

    VAC (voluntary anal contraction) is motor sacral sparing. DAP (deep anal pressure) is sensory sacral sparing. Each is Yes or No.

    Body

    VAC: the external anal sphincter contracts around the examiner's finger on command. DAP: the patient reliably feels firm pressure on the anorectal wall.

    Together with S4-5 light touch and pin prick at the perianal skin, they decide whether the injury is complete.

    What ifs
    What if only DAP is present?

    The injury is incomplete: at least AIS B.

    What if VAC is present?

    Motor incomplete. At least AIS C, whatever the limbs show.

    Classification

    Step 1Sensory level
    Core

    The most caudal dermatome with LT 2 and PP 2, with every dermatome above also normal. One per side.

    Body

    Walk down from C2. The first dermatome with any score below 2, in either modality, ends the walk. The sensory level is the dermatome just above it.

    • C2 already abnormal → sensory level C1.
    • Everything normal through S4-5 → INT (intact).
    What ifs
    What if a normal dermatome appears below an abnormal one?

    Ignore it. The level is set by the first abnormality from the top.

    What if right and left differ?

    Record both. That's why there are four levels (sensory and motor, each side).

    Step 2Motor level
    Core

    The lowest key muscle graded 3 or more, with all key muscles above graded 5. Where there's no key muscle, use the sensory level.

    Body

    Walk the key muscles down. A 5: keep going. A 3 or 4: that's the level. A 0–2: the level is the segment above.

    In regions without a myotome (C1–C4, T2–L1, S2–S5) motor level = sensory level, provided the testable motor function above it is normal.

    • C5 5, C6 4, C7 1 → motor level C6.
    • C5 3, C6 5 → motor level C5 (C6 doesn't count: C5 isn't 5).
    What ifs
    What if C5 is 2 and C2–C4 sensation is intact?

    Motor level C4. C4 counts as normal because its sensation is intact.

    What if all arm muscles are 5 and the sensory level is T6?

    Motor level T6. In the trunk, motor follows sensation.

    What if L2 is 4 but L1 sensation is impaired?

    The walk stops at L1, so the motor level is T12.

    Step 3Neurological level of injury
    Core

    The most rostral of the four levels: sensory right, sensory left, motor right, motor left.

    Body

    The NLI is the most caudal segment with normal sensation and antigravity (≥3) strength on both sides, with normal function above. Taking the highest of the four levels gives exactly that.

    What ifs
    SL R C6, SL L C7, ML R C6, ML L C5. NLI?

    C5, the most rostral of the four.

    Why a single NLI when the sides differ?

    The AIS C/D count uses key muscles below one NLI. The four side levels are still recorded.

    Step 4Complete or incomplete
    Core

    Complete only if VAC No, DAP No, and every S4-5 score is 0 (LT and PP, both sides). Any sacral sparing makes it incomplete.

    Body

    S4-5 is the most caudal segment. Any preserved function there means some pathways cross the lesion, so the injury is incomplete no matter how little the limbs show.

    What ifs
    What if S4-5 pin prick is 1 on one side only?

    Incomplete.

    What if S4-5 is NT and there's no VAC or DAP?

    Completeness can't be decided: ND.

    Step 5AIS grade A–E
    Core

    A complete · B sensory only · C motor, under half ≥3 · D motor, half or more ≥3 · E normal.

    Body
    Complete? VAC No, DAP No, all S4-5 = 0
    Yes → ANo ↓
    Motor complete? No VAC and no motor function more than 3 levels below the motor level on either side
    Yes → BNo ↓
    Half or more of key muscles below the single NLI graded ≥3?
    No → CYes → D
    Sensation and motor normal everywhere, with a documented prior SCI → E
    • B: sensory but not motor function below the NLI including S4-5, and no motor function more than three levels below the motor level on either side.
    • C: motor function preserved: VAC, or sensory sparing plus motor function more than three levels below the motor level on a side (key or non-key muscles).
    • D: motor incomplete as C, with at least half of key muscles below the NLI at grade 3 or more.
    What ifs
    C7 injury, S4-5 sensation spared, and the right ankle plantar flexors flicker (1)?

    S1 is far more than 3 levels below C7, so it's motor incomplete: AIS C.

    Motor function only 2 levels below the motor level?

    Still AIS B. It must be more than 3 levels below.

    How do I count for D at NLI C6?

    Below C6: C7, C8, T1, L2–S1 = 8 per side, 16 total. D needs 8 or more at grade ≥3.

    The patient looks like AIS B. Anything else to test?

    Yes. Test non-key muscles more than 3 levels below the motor level on each side to separate B from C.

    Normal exam with no history of SCI?

    No AIS grade. E applies only to someone with a documented SCI who has recovered.

    Step 6Zone of partial preservation
    Core

    Only when S4-5 function is absent: the most caudal segment with any function, recorded per side for sensory and motor.

    Body
    • Motor ZPP applies when VAC is absent. It's the most caudal key muscle with any movement, never above the motor level.
    • Sensory ZPP applies when DAP is absent and S4-5 has no LT or PP on that side. It's the most caudal dermatome with any sensation, never above the sensory level.
    • If sacral sparing exists, that ZPP is NA.
    What ifs
    Can an AIS B injury have a ZPP?

    A motor ZPP, yes, if VAC is absent. The sensory ZPP is NA because sacral sensation is present.

    What if nothing works below the level?

    ZPP equals the level itself.

    Reference

    ScoresSubscores and totals
    Core

    UEMS 50 · LEMS 50 · LT 112 · PP 112. They track recovery; they don't set the grade.

    Body

    UER and UEL are 25 each, LER and LEL 25 each. LT and PP are 56 per side. Motor is split into upper and lower limbs because a combined 100-point total hides which limb changed.

    What ifs
    What if one muscle is NT?

    That subscore and its total can't be calculated.

    Core

    Used in an apparent AIS B: test non-key muscles more than 3 levels below the motor level on each side to separate B from C.

    Body
    RootMovement
    What ifs
    Do non-key muscles change the motor level?

    No. They only decide B versus C.

    NotationNT, ND and the asterisk
    Core

    NT = a single item couldn't be tested. ND = a classification result can't be determined. * = a non-SCI condition affects the score.

    Body

    Any of the sensory, motor and neurological levels, the AIS grade, and the ZPP can be ND when NT scores block the step. Starred scores need a comment saying whether they count as normal for classification.

    What ifs
    NT below the sensory level. Is the level ND?

    No. The level is set by the first abnormality above the NT segment, so it's still determined.
