    Examination

    Overview

    The worksheet at a glance

    Per side: 10 key muscles graded 0–5 and 28 dermatomes scored 0–2 for light touch and pin prick, plus the anorectal exam (VAC, DAP). The output is four levels, the NLI, completeness, the AIS grade and the ZPP.

    The exam is done supine. The worksheet mirrors the patient: right side on the left of the page, left on the right, C2 to S4-5 down the middle.

    Classification runs in a fixed order, and each step uses only what came before: sensory levels, motor levels, NLI, completeness, AIS grade, ZPP.

    Subscores (UEMS, LEMS, LT and PP totals) track change over time. They never set the AIS grade.

    Score that item NT. If it blocks a step, record that result as ND (not determinable).

    Tag the affected scores with an asterisk and state in the comments whether they count as normal for classification.

    Grading

    Motor grades 0–5

    Grade 3 is full range against gravity. It is the threshold that defines the motor level and separates AIS C from D. Grade 5 is normal.

    GradeDefinition
    0Total paralysis
    1Palpable or visible contraction
    2Active movement, full range of motion with gravity eliminated
    3Active movement, full range against gravity
    4Full range against gravity and moderate resistance in a muscle-specific position
    5Normal: full range against gravity and full resistance in a functional position, as expected in an unimpaired person
    NTNot testable: immobilization, pain preventing grading, amputation, or contracture over 50% of normal range
    0*–4*Non-SCI condition present; explain in comments

    Not a 3; full range is required. If it completes the full range with gravity eliminated, it is a 2.

    NT. The grade can't be judged.

    Record the grade with an asterisk (e.g. 3*) and state whether it should be treated as normal for classification.

    Grading

    Sensory scores 0–2

    Each dermatome gets a light touch and a pin prick score: 0 absent, 1 altered, 2 normal. A dermatome is intact only when both are 2.

    The face (cheek) is the normal reference.

    • 1, altered: decreased, impaired, or hypersensitive.
    • Pin prick tests sharp/dull discrimination. If the patient can't reliably tell sharp from dull, PP is 0.
    • Light touch uses a cotton tip stroked over no more than 1 cm.

    Each modality totals 56 per side, 112 overall.

    LT 2, PP 0. The dermatome isn't intact, so the sensory level stops above it.

    Hypersensitivity is altered sensation: 1.

    NT.

    Examination

    Key muscles

    Ten per side: five in the arm, C5–T1, and five in the leg, L2–S1. Each limb maxes at 25.

    RootMuscle group

    Upper limbs make the UEMS (max 50), lower limbs the LEMS (max 50).

    The motor level in those regions is taken from the sensory level.

    Examination

    Key sensory points

    One point per dermatome, 28 per side from C2 to S4-5. Landmarks: T4 nipple line, T10 umbilicus, T12 inguinal ligament.

    LevelPoint

    On the midclavicular line, divide the xiphisternum (T6) to umbilicus (T10) distance into quarters: T7, T8, T9.

    Examination

    Anorectal exam: VAC and DAP

    VAC, voluntary anal contraction, is motor sacral sparing. DAP, deep anal pressure, is sensory sacral sparing. Each is recorded Yes or No.

    VAC: the external anal sphincter contracts around the examining finger on command. DAP: the patient reliably perceives firm pressure on the anorectal wall.

    With S4-5 light touch and pin prick at the perianal skin, they decide completeness.

    Incomplete: at least AIS B.

    Motor incomplete: at least AIS C, whatever the limbs show.

    Examination

    Non-key muscles

    Tested in an apparent AIS B: function more than 3 levels below the motor level on a side makes it C.

    RootMovement

    No. They only decide B versus C.

    Classification

    Step 1

    Sensory level

    The most caudal dermatome with LT 2 and PP 2, with every dermatome above it also normal. One per side.

    Walk down from C2. The first dermatome with any score below 2, in either modality, ends the walk; the level is the dermatome above it.

    • C2 abnormal: sensory level C1.
    • Normal through S4-5: INT (intact).

    Ignore it. The first abnormality from the top sets the level.

    Record both. That is why there are four levels.

    Step 2

    Motor level

    The lowest key muscle graded 3 or more, with all key muscles above graded 5. Where there is no key muscle, use the sensory level.

    Walk the key muscles down. 5: continue. 3 or 4: that is the level. 0–2: the level is the segment above.

    In regions without a myotome (C1–C4, T2–L1, S2–S5), motor level equals sensory level provided testable motor function above is normal.

    • C5 5, C6 4, C7 1: motor level C6.
    • C5 3, C6 5: motor level C5, because C5 isn't 5.

    Motor level C4. C4 counts as normal because its sensation is intact.

    Motor level T6. In the trunk, motor follows sensation.

    The walk stops at L1, so the motor level is T12.

    Step 3

    Neurological level of injury

    The most rostral of the four levels: sensory right, sensory left, motor right, motor left.

    The NLI is the most caudal segment with normal sensation and antigravity strength on both sides, with normal function above. Taking the highest of the four levels gives exactly that.

    NLI C5.

    The C/D count uses key muscles below one NLI. The four side levels are still recorded.

    Step 4

    Complete or incomplete

    Complete only with VAC No, DAP No, and every S4-5 score 0 (LT and PP, both sides). Any sacral sparing makes it incomplete.

    Preserved function in the most caudal segment means some long-tract continuity across the lesion, however little the limbs show.

    Incomplete.

    Completeness can't be decided: ND.

    Step 5

    AIS grade A–E

    A complete. B sensory incomplete. C motor incomplete, under half of key muscles below the NLI at ≥3. D motor incomplete, half or more at ≥3. E normal.

    Complete?VAC No, DAP No, all S4-5 scores 0

    Yes → ANo ↓

    Motor complete?No VAC, and no motor function more than 3 levels below the motor level on either side

    Yes → BNo ↓

    Half or more of key muscles below the single NLI at grade ≥3?

    No → CYes → D

    All scores normal, after a documented SCI→ E

    • B: sensory but not motor function below the NLI including S4-5, and no motor function more than three levels below the motor level on either side.
    • C: VAC present, or sensory sparing plus motor function more than three levels below the ipsilateral motor level (key or non-key muscles).

    S1 is far more than 3 levels below C7, so it is motor incomplete: C.

    Still B. It must be more than 3 levels below.

    Below C6: C7, C8, T1, L2–S1, 8 per side, 16 total. D needs 8 or more at ≥3.

    Test non-key muscles more than 3 levels below the motor level on each side before calling it B.

    No AIS grade. E applies only after a documented SCI.

    · ·

    Step 6

    Zone of partial preservation

    Only when S4-5 function is absent: the most caudal segment with any function, per side, sensory and motor.

    • Motor ZPP applies when VAC is absent: the most caudal key muscle with any movement, never above the motor level.
    • Sensory ZPP applies when DAP is absent and that side has no S4-5 LT or PP: the most caudal dermatome with any sensation, never above the sensory level.
    • Otherwise NA.

    A motor ZPP, if VAC is absent. The sensory ZPP is NA because sacral sensation is present.

    ZPP equals the level.

    Notation

    Scores

    Subscores and totals

    UEMS 50, LEMS 50, LT 112, PP 112. They track recovery; they don't set the grade.

    UER, UEL, LER, LEL are 25 each; LT and PP are 56 per side. Motor is split by limb because a single 100-point total hides which limb changed.

    That subscore and its total can't be calculated.

    Notation

    NT, ND and the asterisk

    NT: an item couldn't be tested. ND: a result can't be determined. *: a non-SCI condition affects the score.

    Levels, NLI, AIS and ZPP can each be ND when NT scores block the step. Starred scores need a comment on whether they count as normal for classification.

    The level is still determined: the first abnormality above the NT segment sets it.
